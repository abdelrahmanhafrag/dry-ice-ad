§ I · WHAT EVEN IS THIS

Frozen breath,
technically.

Dry ice is solid carbon dioxide. The same gas you exhale, the same gas plants drink for breakfast — just compressed, chilled, and pressed into a brick that refuses to melt.

Each molecule is a single carbon atom double-bonded to two oxygen atoms in a perfectly linear, symmetrical arrangement. Symmetric enough that the dipole moments cancel — making CO₂ a non-polar molecule despite those polar C=O bonds.

Held together not by mighty ionic forces but by the weakest of weak interactions: London dispersion. That's why the solid form is so eager to leave. It barely commits.

§ II · THE MAGIC TRICK

It refuses to melt.

−78.5°C

At sea-level pressure (1 atm), solid CO₂ doesn't melt. It sublimates — going directly from solid to gas, skipping liquid altogether.

The reason is its triple point sits at 5.11 atm, not 1. Anywhere on Earth at normal pressure, the liquid phase is physically impossible. The phase diagram literally has no door for it.

That fog you see at theme parks? Not the CO₂ itself — that's invisible. It's water vapor in the air condensing as the icy gas rolls past. The dry ice does the cooling; your humid breath provides the spectacle.

No Puddles, Ever

Skips the liquid phase entirely. Pack a cooler with dry ice and what you'll find later is… nothing. No sloshing meltwater. No soggy cardboard. Just gas, drifting away to be a cloud's problem.

02

Twice The Cooling

Per kilogram, dry ice absorbs roughly twice the heat of regular water ice as it sublimates. Plus it operates at −78.5°C — about 78 degrees colder than water ice's measly 0°C melting point.

03

Heavier-Than-Air Fog

Gaseous CO₂ has a molar mass of 44 g/mol; air averages ~29 g/mol. The fog produced sinks and creeps along the floor instead of floating away. Theatrical. Atmospheric. Zero CGI required.

§ V · A FEW HONEST WORDS

Side effects of cold.

Dry ice is fantastic. Dry ice is also −78.5°C and produces a gas that displaces oxygen. Read the small print. It's where the chemistry teacher lives.

❄

Will Burn You. Coldly.

Skin contact with dry ice causes cryogenic burns that look identical to thermal burns. Use insulated gloves. Tongs are better. Bare hands are deeply not.

⌬ Tsurface = −78.5°C
☁

Ventilate, Always.

CO₂ is heavier than air. In small or sealed rooms, sublimating dry ice displaces oxygen. At 5–10% atmospheric CO₂, things go bad fast. Open windows. Open everything.

⌬ M(CO₂) = 44 > M(air) ≈ 29
⚡

Never Seal It.

1 kg of solid CO₂ becomes 845 liters of gas. Inside a closed container, that gas has nowhere to go and turns the container into a pressure bomb. Always, always vent.

⌬ Vgas/Vsolid ≈ 845
